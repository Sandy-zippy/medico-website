# CSA Z8000 standard

*Team Medico, November 27, 2026*

CSA Z8000 is the Canadian standard for planning, designing and building health care facilities. This guide is for clinic owners in BC who keep hearing the name from architects, health authorities or the College. You'll find out what it covers, when it is required, and how we use it on a clinic build.

## What is CSA Z8000?

**CSA Z8000, Canadian health care facilities, is CSA Group's national standard for planning, designing and building health care facilities, from hospitals to outpatient clinics.**

The College of Physicians and Surgeons of BC calls it "the nationally recognized standard for the planning, design and construction of health care facilities in Canada" ([CPSBC Facility Design Assessment policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)).

It is written by CSA Group and sold through the CSA store ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)). It sets out requirements for planning, site and facility layout, functional spaces and building systems. In CSA's own summary, it does not set operational models, policies or procedures.

## Is CSA Z8000 mandatory for clinics in BC?

**It is required when a regulator or owner makes it a condition: CPSBC requires it for non-hospital facilities in its accreditation program, and Fraser Health writes it into its design requirements.**

Z8000 is a standard, not a law. It becomes binding in three common ways:

- **CPSBC's accreditation program.** New non-hospital facilities, and existing ones that renovate or change ownership, "are required to be in conformance with the current edition" of Z8000 ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). These are the facilities in the College's Non-Hospital Medical and Surgical Facilities Accreditation Program.
- **Health authority buildings.** Fraser Health's Technical Design Requirements say "all Facilities shall be designed compliant with CSA Z8000 and relevant FHA guidelines" ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf)).
- **Your own lease or funding terms.** A landlord, lender or partner can name it in the agreement.

For a private family practice or dental office outside those programs, we found no BC rule that makes Z8000 compulsory. The BC Building Code and your municipality still apply. Some owners choose to use Z8000 anyway, because it gives the design team one baseline to work from.

## Which facilities does Z8000 cover?

**Hospitals, long-term care and outpatient diagnosis and treatment facilities, public or private, for new builds, temporary facilities and renovations. It does not cover veterinary facilities.**

CSA's summary says the standard applies to facilities "regardless of type, size, location, or range of services." It covers new facilities, temporary facilities, and existing facilities undergoing addition or renovation. It also covers research and testing facilities that perform medical procedures ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)).

It does not apply to veterinary facilities, funeral homes or mobile health units. If you are building a [veterinary clinic](/veterinary-clinic-construction/), your design follows the building code and your college's rules, not Z8000.

## What is CSA Z8000-18, and is there a newer edition?

**Z8000-18 is the 2018 edition, the second edition, which replaced the first edition from 2011. A third edition, CSA Z8000:24, was published in 2024.**

Many people search for "CSA Z8000 18" because the 2018 edition was in use for years. The 2018 edition updated the infection prevention and control requirements and gave more detail on the interdisciplinary design team and its role in planning ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)).

ANSI's webstore lists CSA Z8000:24, the third edition, as the most recent ([ANSI webstore](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz80002024)). The Standards Council of Canada lists it with a date of October 31, 2024 ([SCC standards database](https://ccn-scc.ca/standardsdb/standards/4033548)).

Which edition applies to you depends on who asks for it. CPSBC asks for the edition "in effect at the time of the proposed new construction, renovations or change of ownership" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). Check the edition named in your contract or approval before design starts.

## Where can I get the CSA Z8000 PDF?

**Buy it from the CSA Group store. It is a paid standard, and copies posted on file-sharing sites may be old editions.**

CPSBC hands out its own design assessment tool only after a facility "confirms that they have obtained their own certified copy of the CSA Z8000 standard" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). So if you are going through accreditation, plan on buying it.

You don't need to read all of it yourself. Your architect and engineers work from it. What you need is a team that knows which rooms and systems it touches in your clinic.

## How do we use Z8000 on a clinic build?

**We plan the layout and systems to Z8000 with your architect and engineers, build to the drawings they sign, and follow CSA Z317.13 infection control during construction.**

- **Planning.** We check the edition that applies, then work with your architect and engineers on a Z8000 layout from the first sketch.
- **Drawings.** For facilities in CPSBC's program, the College checks the layout drawings signed by the interdisciplinary design team "prior to building permitting or construction" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)).
- **Construction.** Fraser Health pairs Z8000 with CSA Z317.13 for infection control during construction. Interior Health tells contractors they "must work in compliance with CSA Z317.13-12" ([Interior Health Owners General Requirements, April 2026](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/owners-general-requirements.pdf)). IH's document cites the 2012 edition; the current edition is 2022.
- **Handover.** CPSBC validates full conformance "through letters of assurance and other supporting documentation from the registered professionals" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). We gather the closeout paperwork they need.

See our [medical clinic](/medical-clinic/) and [dental clinic](/dental-clinic/) pages for the kinds of clinics we build.

## What about pharmacy clean rooms?

**Z8000 sets the general planning and design baseline. The numbers for compounding rooms, such as air changes and room pressures, come from NAPRA's model standards.**

In BC, the College of Pharmacists of BC makes the NAPRA sterile compounding standards mandatory through its bylaws. Where a health authority owns the building, its design requirements also apply. Fraser Health, for example, requires its facilities to be designed to Z8000. Our [CSA Z8000 for pharmacy clean rooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/csa-z8000-pharmacy/) page covers compounding room work.

## FAQ

### Is CSA Z8000 the same as the BC Building Code?

No. The BC Building Code (or the Vancouver Building By-law in Vancouver) is law. Z8000 is a CSA standard that adds health care planning and design requirements on top, and it is binding when a regulator, health authority or contract requires it.

### Does Z8000 apply to a dental office?

CSA's summary says the standard applies to health care facilities regardless of type or size, including outpatient treatment facilities. Whether you must follow it depends on who requires it, so check your college, landlord and lender terms.

### Who checks that a clinic meets Z8000?

For facilities in CPSBC's accreditation program, the College checks the layout drawings, reviews the commissioning documents, and looks at finishes and fixtures on site. Full conformance is validated through letters of assurance from the architect and engineers.

### Does Z8000 cover operating a clinic after it opens?

No. CSA says Z8000 does not set requirements for operational models, policies or procedures. CPSBC does require non-hospital facilities to maintain the facility in conformance with Z8000, CSA Z8001 (commissioning) and CSA Z8002 (operation and maintenance).
