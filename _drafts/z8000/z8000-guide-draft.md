# CSA Z8000 standard

*Team Medico, November 27, 2026*

CSA Z8000 is the Canadian standard for planning, designing and building health care facilities. This guide is for clinic owners in BC who keep hearing the name from architects, health authorities or the College. You'll find out what it covers, when it is required, and how we use it on a clinic build.

## What is CSA Z8000?

**CSA Z8000, Canadian health care facilities, is CSA Group's national standard for planning, designing and building health care facilities, from hospitals to outpatient clinics.**

The College of Physicians and Surgeons of BC calls it "the nationally recognized standard for the planning, design and construction of health care facilities in Canada" ([CPSBC Facility Design Assessment policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)).

It is written by CSA Group and sold through the CSA store ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)). It sets out how a facility is planned and laid out, room by room, and how its building systems are designed. In CSA's own summary, it does not set operational models, policies or procedures.

## Is CSA Z8000 mandatory for clinics in BC?

**It is required when a regulator or owner makes it a condition: CPSBC requires it for accredited non-hospital facilities, and health authorities write it into their contracts.**

Z8000 is a standard, not a law. It becomes binding in three common ways:

- **CPSBC accredited facilities.** New non-hospital facilities, and existing ones that renovate or change ownership, "are required to be in conformance with the current edition" of Z8000 ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). These are the facilities in the College's Non-Hospital Medical and Surgical Facilities Accreditation Program.
- **Health authority buildings.** Fraser Health's Technical Design Requirements say "all Facilities shall be designed compliant with CSA Z8000 and relevant FHA guidelines" ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf)).
- **Your own lease or funding terms.** A landlord, lender or partner can name it in the agreement.

For a private family practice or dental office outside those programs, we found no BC rule that makes Z8000 compulsory. The BC Building Code and your municipality still apply. Many owners use Z8000 anyway, because it gives the design team one baseline to work from.

## Which facilities does Z8000 cover?

**Hospitals, long-term care, outpatient clinics and doctors' and dentists' offices, for new builds, temporary facilities and renovations. It does not cover veterinary clinics.**

CSA's summary says the standard applies to facilities "regardless of type, size, location, or range of services." It covers new facilities, temporary facilities, and existing facilities undergoing addition or renovation. It also covers research and testing facilities that perform medical procedures ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)).

It does not apply to veterinary facilities, funeral homes or mobile health units. If you are building a [veterinary clinic](/veterinary-clinic-construction/), your design follows the building code and your college's rules, not Z8000.

## What is CSA Z8000-18, and is there a newer edition?

**Z8000-18 is the 2018 edition, the second after the first one in 2011. CSA's store and ANSI now list a 2024 edition.**

Many people search for "CSA Z8000 18" because the 2018 edition was in use for years. The 2018 edition added infection prevention and control through every project stage, and a bigger role for the design team ([CSA Group store](https://www.csagroup.org/store/product/Z8000/)). ANSI's webstore lists CSA Z8000:24 as the most recent edition ([ANSI webstore](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz80002024)).

Which edition applies to you depends on who asks for it. CPSBC asks for the edition "in effect at the time of the proposed new construction, renovations or change of ownership" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). Check the edition named in your contract or approval before design starts.

## Where can I get the CSA Z8000 PDF?

**Buy it from the CSA Group store. It is a paid standard, and copies posted on file-sharing sites may be old editions.**

CPSBC hands out its own design assessment tool only after a facility "confirms that they have obtained their own certified copy of the CSA Z8000 standard" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). So if you are going through accreditation, plan on buying it.

You don't need to read all of it yourself. Your architect and engineers work from it. What you need is a team that knows which rooms and systems it touches in your clinic.

## How do we use Z8000 on a clinic build?

**We plan the layout and systems to Z8000, build to the drawings your registered professionals sign, and keep the site clean under CSA Z317.13.**

- **Planning.** We check the edition that applies, then lay out rooms, sinks, finishes and clean and dirty flows to it from the first sketch.
- **Drawings.** On accredited facilities, CPSBC checks the layout drawings signed by the interdisciplinary design team "prior to building permitting or construction" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)).
- **Construction.** Health authorities pair Z8000 with CSA Z317.13 for infection control during construction. Interior Health tells contractors they "must work in compliance with CSA Z317.13-12" ([Interior Health Owner's General Requirements, April 2026](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/owners-general-requirements.pdf)).
- **Handover.** CPSBC confirms full conformance "through letters of assurance and other supporting documentation from the registered professionals" ([CPSBC policy](https://www.cpsbc.ca/files/pdf/NHMSFAP-P-Facility-Design-Assessment.pdf)). We gather the closeout paperwork they need.

## What about pharmacy clean rooms?

**Hospital pharmacy clean rooms are built under the health authority's contract, which names Z8000. Compounding room numbers themselves come from NAPRA.**

Fraser Health requires its facilities to be designed to Z8000, and that includes hospital pharmacies. The pharmacy rules for compounding rooms come from NAPRA's model standards, through the provincial college. Our [pharmacy clean rooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/) section covers that work.

## FAQ

### Is CSA Z8000 the same as the BC Building Code?

No. The building code is law and applies to every building. Z8000 is a CSA standard that adds health care planning and design requirements on top, and it is binding when a regulator, health authority or contract requires it.

### Does Z8000 apply to a dental office?

CSA's summary says the standard applies to health care facilities regardless of type or size, including outpatient treatment facilities. Whether you must follow it depends on who requires it, so check your college, landlord and lender terms.

### Who checks that a clinic meets Z8000?

For CPSBC accredited facilities, the College checks the layout drawings, reviews the commissioning documents, and looks at finishes and fixtures on site. Full conformance is confirmed through letters of assurance from the architect and engineers.

### Does Z8000 cover operating a clinic after it opens?

No. CSA says Z8000 does not set operational policies or procedures. CPSBC asks accredited facilities to maintain them in line with Z8000, CSA Z8001 for commissioning and CSA Z8002 for operation and maintenance.
