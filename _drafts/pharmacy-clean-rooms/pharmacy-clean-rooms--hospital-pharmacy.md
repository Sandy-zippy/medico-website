# Hospital pharmacy NAPRA upgrades, IV rooms and sterile compounding suites

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

A hospital pharmacy NAPRA upgrade rebuilds the IV room or sterile compounding suite so its rooms, pressures, air changes and exhaust meet the NAPRA model standards, usually while the pharmacy keeps compounding. Health authorities in BC and Alberta buy this work through prequalification and then a tender or a construction management RFP. This page explains what the work includes, how it is bought, and how we approach it.

## What does a hospital pharmacy NAPRA upgrade include?

**New or rebuilt anterooms and clean rooms, hazardous drug rooms, HVAC and exhaust, pass-throughs, pressure monitoring, and the phasing and temporary space to keep compounding.**

BC tender titles show how health authorities describe the work: "Pharmacy NAPRA Upgrades", "Sterile Compounding", "Pharmacy IV Room Upgrades", "CIVA HVAC and Pass-Through Upgrade", "Pharmacy Hub Negative Air Room" and "Pharmacy HVAC Upgrade".

| Project (as listed) | Value listed | Scope and phasing noted in the listing |
|---|---|---|
| Peace Arch Hospital sterile compounding (Fraser Health) | $6.25M | New hazardous and non-hazardous suites, demolition of the existing suite, structure for overhead equipment; bought as construction management |
| qathet General Hospital IV room upgrades (VCH) | $2.9M (cancelled) | Three conference rooms converted to hazardous and non-hazardous clean rooms, an anteroom and a change room; two phases "to enable the pharmacy to remain operational" |
| Royal Jubilee CIVA HVAC and pass-through upgrade (Island Health) | $1.475M | Up to 3 phases, pharmacy stays open, air changes to NAPRA, electronic differential pressure monitoring |
| Richmond Hospital pharmacy HVAC upgrade | $0.97M | Lab reclassified from non-hazardous to hazardous sterile; supply, return and exhaust upgrades to chemo prep, anteroom and IV rooms |
| UHNBC pharmacy NAPRA upgrades (Northern Health) | $0.5M | Three phases: demolition of the main compounding area with a temporary non-hazardous compounding room, interior work, exterior HVAC |

Source: ConstructConnect listings (links below). Values are indicative and may differ from final contract values.

The room numbers come from NAPRA. For hazardous sterile work the clean room is held at -2.5 Pa, the anteroom at ISO 7 and at least +5.0 Pa to the pharmacy, both rooms get at least 30 air changes an hour, and clean room return air is exhausted outside ([NAPRA hazardous sterile standards](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf)). Our [NAPRA requirements guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/napra-cleanroom-requirements/) and [hazardous drug compounding room](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) page go into detail.

## How do health authorities buy pharmacy compounding projects?

**Through prequalification lists by acute or non-acute category and value, then a stipulated-price tender for smaller upgrades or a construction management RFP for larger suites.**

In the Lower Mainland, Fraser Health, Vancouver Coastal Health and PHSA run one shared, always-open prequalification (RFPQ BC224196) through Bonfire. It qualifies general contractors and construction managers by Acute or Non-Acute category and by value threshold ([CanadaBuys](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/en/tender-opportunities/tender-notice/bc224196)). Project calls then go to prequalified firms only. The Abbotsford sterile compounding design RFP, for example, was open only to architects "prequalified for Acute Care Projects greater than $150K" ([ConstructConnect](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/7522285-rfp-design-sterile-compounding-abbotsford-regional-hospital-and-cancer-centre&find_loc=-V2S-0C2)). A shared Vendor Performance Review scores each firm at contract close, and the score can suspend or advance its status ([Fraser Health VPR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/About-Us/Bids/Introduction-to-VPR-Process-v33----061626.pdf)).

Interior Health keeps its own list in tiers of $200k to $1M, $1M to $5M and over $5M ([Interior Health](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/list-of-pre-qualified-general-contractors.pdf)). Island Health and Northern Health post on bidsandtenders portals.

| Delivery model | Where we have seen it | What it means for the pharmacy team |
|---|---|---|
| Design-bid-build tender | UHNBC, Haida Gwaii, Richmond HVAC | Design is finished first; phasing must be written into the tender |
| Construction management | Peace Arch | The builder joins during design and helps plan phases and shutdowns |
| Design-led RFP | Abbotsford (design stage) | The architect leads; the builder is chosen later |

Our [design-build vs design-bid-build guide](/guides/design-build-vs-design-bid-build/) compares these routes.

## How do you keep a hospital pharmacy compounding during construction?

**Split the work into phases, build and certify a temporary compounding room first, and run infection control to CSA Z317.13 inside the live hospital.**

Three of the five BC listings above (UHNBC, qathet and Royal Jubilee) say the pharmacy must stay operational. UHNBC's listing pairs demolition of the main compounding area with temporary construction of a non-hazardous compounding room. A temporary room used for sterile compounding is itself a compounding area under NAPRA, so we treat it as a real scope item that needs certification before use, and we plan the college notice for it. Health authorities require construction infection control to CSA Z317.13; Interior Health's toolkit sets out hoarding to the underside of deck, HEPA-filtered construction air at 99.97 percent and signed permits for higher preventive measures classes ([Interior Health IPAC toolkit](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/infection-prevention-control-construction-projects.pdf)). Our [hospital pharmacy phasing guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/hospital-pharmacy-phasing/) walks through the options.

## Which standards apply to a hospital compounding suite?

**NAPRA through the provincial college, CSA Z8000, Z317.2 and Z317.13 through the health authority's contract, the building code, and WorkSafeBC hazardous drug rules.**

- **NAPRA.** In BC, the PODSA bylaws (s. 23.7) make the NAPRA sterile standards mandatory ([CPBC](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)).
- **CSA.** Fraser Health requires facilities "designed compliant with CSA Z8000" and work aligned with CSA Z317.13 ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf)). AHS points designers to CSA Z317.2 Table 1 for HVAC by room type and cites Z8000:24 for pharmacy ([AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)). The CSA standards are paywalled; your mechanical engineer works to the current editions. See our [CSA Z8000 guide](/csa-z8000-standard/).
- **WorkSafeBC.** Since December 1, 2023, "a ventilated enclosure must be used for mixing, preparing, and priming specific hazardous drugs" ([WorkSafeBC](https://worksafebc.com/about-us/news-events/announcements/2023/November/new-hazardous-drug-requirements-come-into-effect-december-1)).

## What do we bring to a hospital pharmacy team?

**Sterile suite construction, clean room pod coordination, and a builder's plan for phasing, shutdowns and certification. We have not yet delivered a health authority suite.**

Our sterile work so far is in private pharmacies and compounding labs. In 2020 we built a [sterile suite with two anterooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/coastal-care-sterile-suite/), HEPA laminar-flow diffusers, 30 air changes an hour, interlocked doors, an alarm strobe and low-level exhaust for a hazardous negative-pressure set-up. We are now general contractor on a [modular compounding lab](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/coastal-care-compounding-lab/) (in progress), building the structure for a mezzanine air handler, exhaust, fresh air and electrical service around a factory-built pod. We have also priced a 6,000+ sq ft compounding centre expansion with sterile hazardous and non-hazardous rooms.

On a hospital job, that experience shows up as:
- an early review of exhaust routes, roof work and structure for air handlers, which drive cost and schedule
- a phase plan that starts with the temporary compounding room
- long-lead items (air handlers, doors, pass-throughs) ordered to suit the shutdown windows
- the certifier booked into the schedule, not added at the end ([certification and commissioning](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/))

## FAQ

### Has Medico built a hospital compounding suite?
Not yet. Our completed sterile and hazardous compounding work is in private pharmacies and compounding labs. We say so plainly so you can judge us on process and references.

### Do we need a prequalified contractor?
For Lower Mainland and Interior Health work, yes. Project calls go only to firms prequalified at the right category and value.

### Is a NAPRA upgrade a renovation or a new build?
Usually a renovation inside a live hospital: rebuilding the suite, converting rooms from non-hazardous to hazardous, or replacing HVAC.

### Can a modular clean room be used in a hospital?
Yes. The qathet IV room tender named a modular supplier. See [modular vs built-in](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/modular-vs-built-in/).

### How long does a hospital pharmacy upgrade take?
Peace Arch was bid in May 2026 with construction listed to start in January 2027. See our [timeline guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/cleanroom-project-timeline/).
