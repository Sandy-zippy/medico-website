# CPBC pharmacy renovation approval: the change-of-layout steps for clean rooms and compounding labs

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

In BC, a pharmacy that changes its layout, including adding or rebuilding a clean room, must tell the College of Pharmacists of BC with a Form 8G and a scaled diagram at least 30 days before work starts, then send inspection evidence within 14 days of finishing. A new pharmacy or a move to a new location needs a new licence instead. This guide sets out the steps, the timing, how they fit around construction, and a short note on Alberta.

## When does a pharmacy renovation need college approval in BC?

**When the layout changes. Adding, moving or rebuilding a clean room, compounding room or dispensary is a layout change and goes to the college first.**

The college's change in layout page covers renovations to a licensed pharmacy ([CPBC change in layout](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758)). It has also published a reminder titled "Undergoing Pharmacy Renovations? Don't Forget to Report Layout Changes to the College" ([CPBC](https://bcpharmacists.org/node/2011)). This applies to hospital pharmacies too: the college publishes a hospital pharmacy pre-opening inspection photo checklist ([CPBC hospital checklist](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/7_Forms/7-3_Pharmacy/9120-HOS_PY_Preopening_Inspection_Report_Photo_Checklist.pdf)).

Moving is different. "The pharmacy licence is cancelled when the location of the pharmacy changes," and you must apply "at least 30 days prior" to the move ([CPBC change of location](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1757)). A new pharmacy goes through full licensure; the college's licensure guide estimates 6 to 12 months to open and has its own sterile compounding appendix ([CPBC Pharmacy Licensure Guide](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/3_Registration_Licensure/5237-Pharmacy-Licensure-Guide.pdf)).

## What are the CPBC steps for a change in layout?

**Send Form 8G and a to-scale diagram 30 days before work starts, build, then submit the inspection report and photo evidence within 14 days.**

| Step | When | What you send | College processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Notice of change | No later than 30 days before renovation starts | PODSA Form 8G and the proposed diagram | Up to 7 days |
| 2. Construction | After notice | Build to the diagram you sent | |
| 3. Evidence | No later than 14 days after completion | Inspection report and digital evidence (photos or video) | Up to 14 days |
| 4. Possible on-site inspection | After evidence | Inspector visit, if scheduled | |

Source: [CPBC change in layout](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758), as of November 2026. The college also explains how to fill in the inspection report ([CPBC](https://bcpharmacists.org/node/2209)).

The bylaws require the diagram to be "professionally drawn to scale" ([PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). In practice that means your designer's floor plan, showing each room and what is in it, not a sketch.

## What does the college check in a clean room renovation?

**That the rooms meet the NAPRA sterile standards: pressures, air changes, temperature, continuous monitoring with an alarm, and the right hood, sink, eyewash and pass-through.**

PODSA bylaws s. 23.7 make the NAPRA non-hazardous and hazardous sterile standards mandatory in BC. The college's Sterile Compounding Review form shows what inspectors look at ([CPBC 5287](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf)):

| Item | What the form looks for |
|---|---|
| Anteroom pressure | At least 5.0 Pa, "ideally between 5.0 Pa and 12.5 Pa" |
| Monitoring | "Pressure must be measured continuously, and an alarm system must be in place" |
| Temperature | Controlled rooms "less than or equal to 20°C" |
| Non-hazardous anteroom | At least 20 air changes an hour |
| Sampling | Air and surface sampling "at least every 6 months" |

The hospital photo checklist adds the anteroom demarcation line, a hands-free sink and eyewash, the pass-through, the hood type, hazardous drug storage, an incubator or an external lab report, and a maintenance log with hood maintenance and certification ([CPBC hospital checklist](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/7_Forms/7-3_Pharmacy/9120-HOS_PY_Preopening_Inspection_Report_Photo_Checklist.pdf)). Plan the photos as you build, since some items are hidden once ceilings close. Our [NAPRA requirements guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/napra-cleanroom-requirements/) lists the room numbers.

## How do college steps fit with permits and construction?

**Run them in parallel. Permit, landlord or strata review and the college notice each have their own clock, and certification must finish before evidence.**

The college notice is not a building permit. The city or township issues the permit separately, and on our projects it has brought extra steps: one township needed a separate sprinkler permit and engineer seismic sign-off, and a business park strata review sat between permit and tender on a compounding lab we are building now. Health authority projects add their own internal reviews.

| Track | Typical trigger | Who to plan with |
|---|---|---|
| CPBC Form 8G | 30 days before work starts | Pharmacy manager and designer |
| Building permit | Before construction | Designer and engineers |
| Landlord or strata review | Before tender or permit | Owner |
| Certification | After commissioning, before evidence | Certifier and builder ([certification and commissioning](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/)) |
| CPBC evidence | Within 14 days of completion | Pharmacy manager |

Because the evidence is due within 14 days of completion, book the certifier well ahead. A failed pressure or particle test pushes the whole filing back. For hospital work done in phases, each phase that changes a compounding room, including a temporary room, may need its own pass through these steps. Our [phasing guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/hospital-pharmacy-phasing/) covers that.

## What about pharmacy renovation approval in Alberta?

**The Alberta College of Pharmacy also requires approval for pharmacy renovations. Check its current process early, since its steps and forms differ from BC's.**

The Alberta College of Pharmacy has published that "pharmacy renovations require approval" ([ACP](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/pharmacy-renovations-require-approval-acp/)), and it publishes its own guidance on sterile and non-sterile compounding ([ACP sterile compounding](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/does-your-pharmacy-provide-sterile-compounding/); [ACP non-sterile FAQ](https://abpharmacy.ca/wp-content/uploads/Compounding_Non-sterileFAQ.pdf)). The NAPRA room numbers are national, but timing and forms are set by each college. For Alberta health authority projects, AHS design requirements also apply ([AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)).

## FAQ

### How far ahead must we notify CPBC of a renovation?
At least 30 days before the renovation starts, using Form 8G and the proposed diagram.

### Do we need college approval for a non-sterile compounding room?
A new or changed room is still a layout change, so file Form 8G. BC has not adopted the NAPRA non-sterile standard in bylaw, so confirm room requirements with the college.

### Does a hospital pharmacy follow the same steps?
Yes, hospital pharmacies are licensed by the college, which publishes a hospital pre-opening photo checklist.

### What if the work finishes late?
The 14-day evidence clock starts at completion, not at your planned date. Check with the college if your dates change.

### Is relocating a pharmacy a layout change?
No. A move cancels the licence, and you apply for a new one at least 30 days before moving.
