# CSA Z8000 pharmacy clean rooms and compounding labs

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

CSA Z8000 is the Canadian standard for planning, designing and building health care facilities, and it includes a pharmacy clause. Health authority design requirements call up CSA Z8000 and its companions, Z317.2 (HVAC) and Z317.13 (infection control during construction), for the facilities they build and renovate, and the NAPRA compounding standards apply through the provincial college. This page explains which standard does what, who must follow it, and how we work with it.

## What is CSA Z8000, and does it cover pharmacies?

**CSA Z8000 is the national baseline for planning, designing and building health care facilities, and it has its own pharmacy clause that health authorities cite.**

CSA describes Z8000 as "a comprehensive, nationally-recognized baseline for the planning, design, and construction of hospitals" ([CSA Group](https://www.csagroup.org/article/13-changes-csa-z8000-canadian-healthcare-facilities-know)). The 2024 edition, Z8000:24, covers new and temporary facilities and existing facilities undergoing addition or renovation, both "public or private" ([ANSI webstore](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz80002024)).

Pharmacy is part of it. Alberta Health Services' design requirements point to clause 10.9.3.4 of Z8000:24 for pharmacy, and cite Z8000:24 10.9.2.3 for a dedicated pharmacy staff washroom ([AHS IPC design requirements](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)). The standard itself is sold by CSA and its clause text is not public, so we do not quote clause values on this page. Your design team works from the purchased edition.

For the standard in general, see our [CSA Z8000 guide](/csa-z8000-standard/).

## Which CSA standards apply to a pharmacy clean room?

**Three work together on health authority jobs: Z8000 for the facility, Z317.2 for the HVAC, and Z317.13 for infection control while you build.**

| Standard | What it covers | Where it shows up in a pharmacy clean room job |
|---|---|---|
| CSA Z8000:24, Canadian health care facilities | Planning, design and construction of health care facilities, including a pharmacy clause (10.9.3.4) | The facility planning and design baseline the health authority checks the design against; AHS cites clause 10.9.3.4 for pharmacy and 10.9.2.3 for a pharmacy staff washroom |
| CSA Z317.2:24, HVAC in health care facilities | HVAC planning, design, construction, commissioning, operation and maintenance; Table 1 gives design values by room type | HVAC design values by room type (Table 1) in the mechanical design; AHS tells designers to use Table 1 for each room type |
| CSA Z317.13:22, infection control during construction | How to build, renovate and maintain inside a working health care facility | Hoarding, dust and air control while the hospital pharmacy keeps running |
| NAPRA model standards (through the provincial college) | The compounding room numbers: ISO classes, air changes, pressures, exhaust, hazardous drug storage | What the College reviews, and may inspect, when the new rooms are complete |

Sources: [ANSI Z8000:24](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz80002024), [ANSI Z317.2:24](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz3172024), [AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf), [Interior Health IPAC toolkit](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/infection-prevention-control-construction-projects.pdf).

The 2024 edition of Z317.2 added "more stringent requirements for areas in a facility used for more than one function", revised the rules for HVAC upgrades done as part of renovations, and updated Table 1 ([ANSI Z317.2:24](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/csa/csaz3172024)). Both points matter for pharmacy renovations inside older hospitals.

## Who has to build to Z8000?

**Health authorities require it by contract. Private compounding pharmacies: CSA is not law unless a contract or the authority having jurisdiction calls it up.**

- **Fraser Health.** Its Technical Design Requirements say facilities must be "designed compliant with CSA Z8000 and relevant FHA guidelines" and that "Work must align with CSA Z8000, CSA Z317.13, and the BC Building Code". They apply to "architects, engineers, contractors, facility administrators or operators" ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/about-us/business-opportunities/Technical-Design-Requirements-TDR); quotes from the [combined TDR sections](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf)).
- **Interior Health.** Its staff safety design guidelines, in the pharmacy section, tell designers to "Refer to CSA Standard Z8000-11 and ensure 2013 Canadian Pharmacy standards are incorporated" for pharmacy; they still cite the 2011 edition ([Interior Health](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/staff-safety-guidelines-for-healthcare-facility-design-projects.pdf)). Its contractor requirements make Z317.13 mandatory ([Interior Health OGR](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/owners-general-requirements.pdf)).
- **Alberta Health Services.** Its design requirements cite Z8000:24 for pharmacy, list "sterile compounding pharmacies" as a restricted area, and point to Z317.2 Table 1 for HVAC ([AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)).
- **Private compounding pharmacies and central labs.** Z8000's scope includes private facilities, but the binding rules are NAPRA through the provincial college, the building code and provincial workplace safety rules (WorkSafeBC in BC). A private owner can still choose Z8000 as its design baseline, and we can build to it.

## How do Z8000 and NAPRA fit together?

**NAPRA sets the compounding room numbers the College checks. Z8000 and Z317.2 set the facility and HVAC baseline. Designers typically apply both.**

NAPRA is where the clean room numbers come from: ISO 7 clean rooms with ISO 5 hoods, at least 30 air changes an hour, and negative-pressure hazardous clean rooms behind a positive-pressure anteroom. In BC the College of Pharmacists made the NAPRA sterile standards mandatory through the PODSA bylaws, section 23.7 ([CPBC](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). In Alberta, the College of Pharmacy approved the NAPRA sterile standards, with amendments, and they have been in effect since July 1, 2021 ([ACP](https://abpharmacy.ca/regulated-members/practice-resources/pharmacy-compounding/sterile-compounding/)). Our [NAPRA cleanroom requirements guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/napra-cleanroom-requirements/) has the full numbers.

On a hospital job the mechanical engineer typically works to both NAPRA and Z317.2 Table 1 and uses whichever is stricter, while the construction team works to Z317.13 so the rest of the pharmacy keeps running. That overlap is where hospital pharmacy jobs get hard, and it is why we plan the phasing before we price. See [hospital pharmacy renovation phasing](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/hospital-pharmacy-phasing/).

## FAQ

**Is CSA Z8000 mandatory for a pharmacy clean room?**
On health authority projects, yes, because the authority's design requirements call it up. For a private compounding pharmacy it is not law unless a contract or the authority having jurisdiction requires it; NAPRA, the building code and provincial workplace safety rules (WorkSafeBC in BC) are the binding rules.

**Does Z8000 set the clean room air changes and pressures?**
The compounding room numbers come from NAPRA. HVAC values by room type are in CSA Z317.2 Table 1, which health authorities tell designers to use. Designers typically apply both and use the stricter value.

**Is a compounding lab the same as a clean room?**
Not always. A clean room is the sterile part. A compounding lab can hold sterile clean rooms, non-sterile compounding rooms, or both. See [compounding lab design](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/non-sterile-compounding-lab/).

**Which Z8000 clause covers pharmacy?**
Alberta Health Services cites Z8000:24 clause 10.9.3.4 for pharmacy. The clause text is sold by CSA, so check the current edition with your designer.
