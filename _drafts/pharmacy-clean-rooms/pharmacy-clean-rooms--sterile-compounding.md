# Sterile compounding rooms built to NAPRA standards

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

A sterile compounding room is an ISO Class 7 clean room entered through an anteroom, fed with HEPA-filtered air, held in a pressure cascade and watched by continuous pressure alarms. We build these rooms and full sterile compounding suites for hospital pharmacy teams and for private compounding pharmacies in BC and Alberta. This page covers what the room needs, how a project runs and what we have built.

## What does a sterile compounding room need?

**An ISO 7 clean room, an anteroom, HEPA supply air, a pressure cascade, low-wall returns, a certified PEC and alarmed pressure monitoring.**

The numbers come from the NAPRA model standards, not from the building code. In BC, PODSA bylaw s. 23.7 makes the NAPRA non-hazardous and hazardous sterile standards mandatory for pharmacy owners and managers ([CPBC PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). The College of Pharmacists of BC inspects against its Sterile Compounding Review form, which turns those standards into items an inspector can check on site ([CPBC review form](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf)).

**What a non-hazardous sterile suite has to deliver**

| Item | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Clean room air | ISO Class 7 | CPBC review form |
| Anteroom air | ISO Class 8 for a non-hazardous anteroom | CPBC review form |
| Anteroom pressure | At least +5.0 Pa, ideally 5.0 to 12.5 Pa | CPBC review form |
| Anteroom air changes | At least 20 ACPH | CPBC review form |
| Clean room air changes | At least 30 ACPH | CPBC review form |
| Temperature | 20°C or less in controlled rooms | CPBC review form |
| Pressure monitoring | Measured continuously, with an alarm system | CPBC review form |
| Air path | Terminal HEPA filters, low-wall returns | CPBC review form |
| Finishes | Coved floors 10 to 15 cm up the wall | CPBC review form |
| Water | No sinks in the clean room; hands-free sink in the anteroom | NAPRA, CPBC checklist |
| PEC | Laminar airflow workbench (LAFW) or compounding aseptic isolator (CAI) | CPBC pre-opening checklist |
| Recertification | Air and surface sampling at least every 6 months | CPBC review form |

As of November 2026. Confirm values against the current NAPRA standard and CPBC forms with your engineer before design.

The College's hospital pre-opening photo checklist also asks for an anteroom demarcation line, a pass-through, an eyewash, the PEC type and a maintenance log ([CPBC checklist](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/7_Forms/7-3_Pharmacy/9120-HOS_PY_Preopening_Inspection_Report_Photo_Checklist.pdf)). Each one is a construction item, so we put them on the drawings from the first issue.

## How is a non-hazardous suite different from a hazardous one?

**A non-hazardous clean room is held positive to protect the product. A hazardous room is held negative to protect staff, with outside exhaust.**

In a non-hazardous suite, air pushes out from the clean room through the anteroom to the pharmacy. In a hazardous suite the clean room sits at -2.5 Pa, its anteroom moves up to ISO 7, both rooms need at least 30 ACPH, and the clean room's return air is exhausted outside, low on the walls ([NAPRA hazardous sterile standard](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf)). Most hospital IV rooms and many private labs need both, and NAPRA requires two clean rooms when a site does both kinds of work. Our [hazardous drug compounding room](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) page covers the negative side.

## What does a sterile compounding project involve?

**Gap analysis, design to NAPRA, College notice before work, construction, commissioning and balancing, then certification and post-construction evidence to the College.**

| Step | What happens | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Gap analysis and program | Compare the current or proposed rooms to NAPRA and list the rooms needed | Pharmacy team, design team, us |
| 2. Design | Room layout, pressure cascade, ACPH, exhaust, monitoring, finishes | Architect or designer, mechanical engineer |
| 3. College notice | In BC, Form 8G and the diagram at least 30 days before work starts | Pharmacy manager, with our drawings |
| 4. Construction | Walls, ceilings, HVAC, exhaust, doors, pass-through, controls | Us |
| 5. Commissioning | Air balancing, controls and alarm testing | Mechanical trades, commissioning agent |
| 6. Certification | Room and PEC certification, then baseline sampling | Independent certifier |
| 7. College evidence | Inspection report and photos within 14 days of completion | Pharmacy manager |

Sources: [CPBC change in layout](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758), [CPBC review form](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf). In Alberta, the college also requires approval before a pharmacy renovation ([ACP](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/pharmacy-renovations-require-approval-acp/)).

We bring the certifier in early, because the room is certified under working conditions and the design, the balancing and the test plan have to line up. Our [certification and commissioning](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/) page covers that stage, and the [cleanroom project timeline](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/cleanroom-project-timeline/) guide shows how long each step tends to take.

## Who buys sterile compounding suites, and how?

**Two groups: health authority pharmacy teams buying through tenders and construction management, and private pharmacy owners who hire a builder or design-build team directly.**

Health authorities buy this work as public projects. Listed BC examples include Peace Arch Hospital's sterile compounding suites at $6.25M, bought as construction management, ([Peace Arch listing](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/7522252-rfp-construction-management-services-for-the-peace-arch-hospital-sterile-compound&find_loc=-V4B-2R4)), and smaller "Pharmacy NAPRA Upgrades" tendered at $0.35M to $1.0M ([Haida Gwaii](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/6597349-rfp-design-daajing-giids-pharmacy-napra-standard-upgrades&find_loc=-V0T-1S0); [Prince Rupert](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/5971815-prince-rupert-regional-hospital-pharmacy-napra-upgrade&find_loc=BC-V8J-2A6)). These jobs are phased so the pharmacy keeps compounding. Our [hospital pharmacy NAPRA upgrade](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hospital-pharmacy/) page covers that route.

Private owners usually start with a lease or a building and a list of preparations. For them, the clean room sits inside a wider [compounding pharmacy design-build](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/compounding-pharmacy-design-build/) project, often with non-sterile rooms beside it.

## FAQ

### What ISO class does a sterile compounding room need?
The clean room is ISO Class 7. A non-hazardous anteroom is ISO Class 8 and a hazardous anteroom is ISO Class 7, per the CPBC Sterile Compounding Review form.

### Can a sink go in the clean room?
No. NAPRA says water sources, sinks and drains must not be in a clean room. They are allowed in the anteroom, where the College expects a hands-free sink.

### How often is a sterile compounding room recertified?
The PEC is certified every 6 months, and air and surface sampling is done at least every 6 months. Pressure instruments are calibrated at least once a year.

### Do I need College approval before building?
In BC, yes. Submit Form 8G and the proposed diagram at least 30 days before the renovation starts, then evidence within 14 days of completion. Alberta also requires approval.

### Is a modular clean room allowed?
NAPRA sets performance, not a construction method. Modular and built-in rooms both have to pass the same certification. See our [modular vs built-in](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/modular-vs-built-in/) comparison.
