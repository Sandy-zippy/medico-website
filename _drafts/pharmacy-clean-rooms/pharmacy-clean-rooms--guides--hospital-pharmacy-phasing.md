# Hospital pharmacy renovation phasing: how to keep compounding during a NAPRA upgrade

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

A hospital pharmacy cannot stop making IV, oncology and TPN preparations while its clean rooms are rebuilt, so the work is split into phases, and compounding usually moves to a temporary room or another site first. Most BC health authority tenders for pharmacy NAPRA upgrades are written in two or three phases for this reason. This guide covers the options, a typical phase plan, infection control, and what to settle before tender.

## Why do hospital pharmacy renovations need phasing?

**Because sterile and hazardous compounding must continue for patients every day, and the rooms being rebuilt are the only rooms allowed to make those preparations.**

The tenders say it directly. The UHNBC pharmacy NAPRA upgrade was split into three phases, starting with demolition of the main compounding area and a temporary non-hazardous compounding room, because "The Pharmacy Suite will need to remain active during construction" ([ConstructConnect, UHNBC](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/6474578-university-hospital-of-northern-british-columbia-uhn-pharmacy-napra-upgrades&find_loc=-V2M-1S2)). The qathet IV room project was planned in two phases "to enable the pharmacy to remain operational" ([ConstructConnect, qathet](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/5793237-vch-qathet-general-hospital-powell-river-pharmacy-iv-room-upgrades&find_loc=BC-V8A-5R3)). The Royal Jubilee CIVA HVAC and pass-through upgrade allowed up to three phases with the pharmacy open ([ConstructConnect, Royal Jubilee](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/6193566-royal-jubilee-pharmacy-civa-hvac-and-pass-through-upgrade&find_loc=-V8R-1J8)). At Nanaimo Regional General Hospital, "the pharmacy will remain open throughout the construction period" ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2016/01/06/nanaimo-pharmacy-project-to-enhance-safety/)).

## What are the options for keeping compounding going?

**A certified temporary clean room on site, a mobile unit, another site or central service, or relocating so the old suite runs until switchover.**

| Option | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary clean room on site | Build and certify a small compounding room first, then demolish the old suite | It is a real compounding area: it needs certification before use and, in BC, the college layout steps |
| Mobile clean room unit | A trailer-mounted unit parked next to the hospital | Power, exhaust and site access; mostly a US practice so far ([Germfree](https://www.germfree.com/insights/custom-mobile-pharmacy-cleanroom/)) |
| Another site or central service | Another hospital or a hub makes the preparations for a period | Delivery times and beyond-use dates; SHN used temporary space and a hub-and-spoke set-up ([SHN](https://shn.ca/?p=9526)) |
| Relocate the suite | Build the new suite in different space; the old one runs until switchover | Needs spare space; one US case chose relocation over phased renovation ([McCownGordon](https://kansas.mccowngordon.com/?p=9722)) |

A US regulator, the Minnesota Board of Pharmacy, lists similar choices: a temporary cleanroom certified "prior to use", an approved satellite, outsourcing or a central service, or a mobile unit ([Minnesota Board of Pharmacy](https://mn.gov/boards/assets/SterileCompoundingAreaRemodelGuidancePharmacy%2010.28.2021_tcm21-506511.pdf)). Its rules are US rules, but the options carry over.

## What does a typical phase plan look like?

**Phase one builds the temporary room, phase two rebuilds the suite inside hoarding, and a final phase finishes exterior HVAC, commissioning and certification before switchover.**

This sequence follows the UHNBC tender, which ran demolition of the main compounding area with a temporary non-hazardous compounding room, then interior work, then exterior HVAC.

| Phase | Work | Gate before the next phase |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Planning | Survey, phase drawings, shutdown list, infection control risk assessment | Pharmacy, facilities and infection prevention sign off the plan |
| 1. Temporary room | Build, commission and certify the temporary compounding room | Certification passed; college steps complete; compounding moved |
| 2. Suite rebuild | Demolish and rebuild clean rooms and anterooms inside hoarding | Rough-in inspections; services tied in during agreed shutdowns |
| 3. HVAC and exterior | Air handlers, exhaust stacks and roof work | Air balancing and pressure tests |
| 4. Commission and certify | Controls, alarms, room and hood certification, environmental sampling | Results reviewed by pharmacy; college evidence filed |
| 5. Switchover | Move compounding back; remove the temporary room | New suite in use |

Each phase needs its own pressure and air plan, because the rooms around the work keep running. Exhaust and roof work for air handlers often drive the longest shutdowns. The Peace Arch scope included structural work for overhead equipment ([ConstructConnect, Peace Arch](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/7522252-rfp-construction-management-services-for-the-peace-arch-hospital-sterile-compound&find_loc=-V4B-2R4)).

## How is infection control handled during a pharmacy renovation?

**To CSA Z317.13, using a preventive measures class. Work next to a running compounding suite usually needs sealed hoarding, HEPA-filtered air, permits and daily monitoring.**

Fraser Health and Interior Health both require CSA Z317.13 on construction ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf); [Interior Health](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/owners-general-requirements.pdf)). Interior Health's toolkit uses the Z317.13 preventive measures classes I to IV. Higher classes need an infection control measures permit, signed and posted. Hoarding runs from floor to the underside of the deck, construction air units use HEPA at 99.97 percent, and the constructor gives at least five business days' notice for a permit ([Interior Health IPAC toolkit](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/infection-prevention-control-construction-projects.pdf)). PICNet BC rates projects by construction type against patient risk group to set the class ([PICNet](https://www.picnet.ca/wp-content/uploads/10-Construction-Renovation.pdf)).

In our view, a clean room renovation beside an operating suite will normally sit in a high class. Plan for it in the schedule and the price, and coordinate with the pharmacy's own environmental monitoring so construction dust does not show up in their samples.

## What should be settled before the project goes to tender?

**The temporary compounding plan, the phase drawings, shutdown windows, who supplies the clean room and hoods, and how certification fits each phase.**

- **Temporary space.** Where it goes, who designs it, and how it is certified.
- **Shutdown windows.** Agreed hours for tie-ins to air handlers, exhaust and power. Interior Health's general requirements make overtime from after-hours work the contractor's cost ([Interior Health](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/owners-general-requirements.pdf)), so say clearly when after-hours work is expected.
- **Owner-supplied items.** Hoods, fridges and any modular clean room supplier; the Peace Arch scope required coordination with health authority vendors and equipment suppliers.
- **Long-lead items.** One US builder plans air handlers at about 12 weeks and FRP doors and pass-throughs at 10 to 12 weeks ([JP Cullen](https://jpcullen.com/?p=2689)).
- **Modular or built-in.** Modular panels can cut wet trades and dust inside the hoarding. See [modular vs built-in](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/modular-vs-built-in/).

On larger, more complex suites, construction management lets the builder help write this plan during design. Peace Arch was bought as construction management. See our [design-build vs design-bid-build guide](/guides/design-build-vs-design-bid-build/).

## FAQ

### Does the temporary compounding room need certification?
Yes. It is used for sterile compounding, so it must meet NAPRA and be certified before use, like any clean room.

### How many phases does a pharmacy NAPRA upgrade need?
BC tenders we reviewed used two or three. The number depends on how much space you have for temporary rooms.

### Who decides the infection control class?
The health authority's infection prevention team, with the project team, using CSA Z317.13.

### Can we skip the temporary room and outsource?
Sometimes. Another site or a central service can make preparations for a period, if delivery times and beyond-use dates work.

### How long does phasing add?
It depends on the plan. The SHN Scarborough clean room took about two years of planning, and construction ran from January 2019 to a July 2020 opening ([SHN](https://shn.ca/?p=9526)).
