# Pharmacy clean room construction for hospitals and compounding pharmacies

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

We build pharmacy clean rooms, sterile compounding suites, hazardous drug compounding rooms and non-sterile compounding labs in BC and Alberta. We work for two kinds of owners: hospital and health authority pharmacy teams upgrading IV rooms and compounding suites to NAPRA, and owners of private compounding pharmacies and central compounding facilities. This page explains what we build, the rules that shape each room, and where to start.

## Who do we build pharmacy clean rooms for?

**Hospital and health authority pharmacy teams buying NAPRA upgrades and IV rooms, and owners of private compounding pharmacies and central compounding facilities building new labs.**

The two groups buy in very different ways, so we treat each as its own door into this section.

| | Hospital and health authority pharmacy | Private compounding pharmacy or central facility |

## What rooms does a pharmacy compounding facility need?

**It depends on what you compound. Sterile work needs ISO 7 clean rooms behind anterooms; hazardous work adds negative pressure; non-sterile work needs dedicated rooms.**

A "clean room" is the sterile part of the facility. A compounding lab can hold sterile clean rooms, non-sterile compounding rooms, or both, plus gowning, storage and support space.

| Room | What it is for | Key build points | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-hazardous sterile clean room | IV admixtures, TPN and other sterile preparations | ISO 7 air, positive pressure cascade, HEPA supply, anteroom | [Sterile compounding rooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/sterile-compounding/) |
| Hazardous drug clean room | Chemotherapy and other hazardous sterile preparations | -2.5 Pa to the anteroom, at least 30 air changes an hour, return air exhausted outside, low on the walls | [Hazardous drug compounding rooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) |
| Hazardous drug storage room | Storing hazardous drugs | -2.5 Pa, at least 12 air changes an hour, all air exhausted outside | [Hazardous drug compounding rooms](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) |
| Non-sterile compounding room (Levels A, B, C) | Creams, capsules, hormones, hazardous non-sterile work | From a designated area (Level A) to a separate negative-pressure room with external venting and an eyewash (Level C) | [Compounding lab design](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/non-sterile-compounding-lab/) |

Sources: NAPRA hazardous sterile model standards; NAPRA non-sterile model standards (2018, clarified 2022). Our [NAPRA cleanroom requirements guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/napra-cleanroom-requirements/) has the full numbers.

## Which rules apply to a pharmacy clean room in Canada?

**NAPRA model standards set the room numbers, provincial colleges enforce them, and health authorities add CSA Z8000, Z317.2 and Z317.13 through their contracts.**

In BC, the PODSA bylaws (s. 23.7) require owners and managers to follow the NAPRA non-hazardous and hazardous sterile compounding standards ([CPBC PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). BC has not adopted the NAPRA non-sterile standard in bylaw. NAPRA consulted on new combined compounding documents in early 2026 and its Board has accepted them, but NAPRA says publication "does not mean that they are in effect" ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/update-on-napras-model-documents-for-pharmacy-regulatory-authority-use-on-non-sterile-and-sterile-compounding-standards/)).

Health authorities write CSA standards into their contracts. Fraser Health's Technical Design Requirements say "Work must align with CSA Z8000, CSA Z317.13, and the BC Building Code" ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf)). Alberta Health Services cites CSA Z8000:24 for pharmacy and tells designers to use CSA Z317.2 Table 1 for HVAC by room type ([AHS IPC design requirements](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)). Our [CSA Z8000 guide](/csa-z8000-standard/) explains that standard. US terms such as USP 797 and buffer room appear in a lot of vendor material; our [USP 797 vs NAPRA guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/usp-797-vs-napra/) explains what applies here.

## How does a pharmacy clean room project run?

**Gap analysis and design, college notice, construction, commissioning, certification, then the college's change-of-layout or pre-opening evidence before compounding starts in the new rooms.**

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Gap analysis and program | Compare the current rooms with NAPRA and list what must change |
| 2. Design | Room layout, pressure cascade, air changes, exhaust, monitoring and alarms |
| 3. Approvals | Building permit, and in BC a CPBC Form 8G at least 30 days before work starts ([CPBC](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758)) |
| 4. Construction | Phased if the pharmacy must stay open, with infection control in hospitals |
| 5. Commissioning | Air balancing, controls, pressure monitoring and alarm tests |
| 6. Certification | Room and hood certification by a third-party certifier |
| 7. College evidence | In BC, submit evidence within 14 days of completion |

## Should the clean room be modular or built in place?

**Either can meet NAPRA. Modular rooms install faster with less dust; built-in rooms fit odd spaces. HVAC, exhaust and structure are site-built either way.**

We are general contractor on a project now that installs a factory-built modular clean room pod inside a light-industrial unit, with our team building the structure, services and exhaust around it. We also built a stick-built sterile suite in 2020. Our [modular vs built-in comparison](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/modular-vs-built-in/) sets out the trade-offs.

## FAQ

### Do you build hospital pharmacy clean rooms?
Yes, this is a service we offer. Our completed sterile work so far is in private pharmacies and compounding labs. Our [hospital pharmacy page](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hospital-pharmacy/) explains how we approach health authority jobs.

### Do you work outside BC?
Yes. We work in BC and Alberta. The NAPRA numbers are national; each provincial college sets its own approval steps.

### What is the difference between a clean room and a compounding lab?
A clean room is a sterile room with classified air. A compounding lab is the whole facility, which may include clean rooms, non-sterile rooms and support space.

### How much does a pharmacy clean room cost?
It depends mostly on HVAC, which US sources put at about 35 to 55 percent of a cleanroom budget. See our [cost guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/pharmacy-clean-room-cost/).

### Who certifies the clean room?
An independent certifier. NAPRA requires hoods to be certified every 6 months to CETA guides. See [certification and commissioning](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/).

### Can we keep compounding during the renovation?
Usually, with phasing and a temporary compounding room. See our [hospital phasing guide](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/hospital-pharmacy-phasing/).
