# Coastal Care Pharmacy sterile suite, Surrey

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

In 2020 we built a sterile compounding suite for hazardous preparations at Coastal Care Pharmacy in Surrey, along with a non-sterile compounding room. The suite has a buffer room reached through two anterooms, HEPA laminar-flow diffusers, 30 air changes an hour, interlocked doors and a negative-pressure clean room with low-level exhaust. We were construction manager, building an interior designer's drawings.

## What did Coastal Care Pharmacy need?

**A full-service pharmacy with a secured dispensary, a non-sterile compounding room and a NAPRA sterile suite for hazardous preparations, inside a new building.**

Coastal Care took about 1,950 sq ft of a larger fit-out it shares with a primary care clinic. It was the first tenant fit-out in a new single-storey commercial building, with four retail units combined into one suite. The pharmacy needed retail space, a dispensary and counselling room, and a compounding area that could handle both non-sterile and hazardous sterile work. The wider pharmacy is covered in our [Coastal Care Pharmacy case study](/portfolio-2/coastal-care-pharmacy/).

## How is the sterile suite laid out?

**Staff pass through two anterooms with interlocked doors into a buffer room, where hazardous sterile preparations are made under HEPA laminar-flow air.**

| Room or system | What it has |
|---|---|
| Anteroom 1 | Held positive to the pharmacy; ISO Class 7; interlocked doors |
| Anteroom 2 | Second anteroom in the suite, with its own exhaust fan |
| Buffer (clean) room | ISO Class 7; held negative to Anteroom 1; hood on a stand; HEPA laminar-flow diffusers |
| Suite fittings | Eyewash, gowning bench, touchless sink, FRP walls, pressure displays |
| Controls | Stand-alone controls; shutdown and red strobe outside the anteroom on any failure |
| Non-sterile compounding room | Hood, two-bowl sink, dishwasher, drywall ceiling, dedicated exhaust fan |

The compounding rooms sit together along the rear wall, so plumbing and exhaust runs stay short and the sanitary lines could run under the slab.

## How does the air system work?

**The clean room is held at -2.5 Pa to its anteroom and the anteroom at least +5 Pa to the pharmacy, at 30 air changes.**

That cascade is the one NAPRA's hazardous sterile standard describes: an anteroom "at least 5.0 Pa relative to the pharmacy," and a clean room at "-2.5 Pa" ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf)). Air enters through 24 by 48 in HEPA laminar-flow diffusers. The clean room and each anteroom have their own exhaust fan.

Exhaust is drawn only from grilles 6 in above the floor. NAPRA's standard says clean room return air must go outdoors, and the Saskatchewan college's summary chart notes it leaves at the bottom of the walls. Low exhaust needs space inside the wall for the duct, so the clean room walls were made 6 in deep.

The controls were built to fail safe. If any part of the system fails, it shuts down and a red strobe flashes outside the anteroom. The College of Pharmacists of BC review form also says "Pressure must be measured continuously, and an alarm system must be in place" ([CPBC](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf)).

## What was our role, and how long did it take?

**We were construction manager on the interior designer's drawings. Contracts were signed in May 2020, and construction took about 16 weeks.**

The project was tendered in March 2020. Contracts were signed on May 6. Final electrical and plumbing approvals came in late August, and the final building review was in September 2020. We ran the pharmacy and the clinic as separate contracts in one build, with a clear line between the two tenants on the floor plan.

## What would we tell another pharmacy planning a sterile suite?

**Size the clean room walls for low-level exhaust in the first drawing issue, and settle security, automation and roof work before tender.**

- **Exhaust first.** The low-level exhaust route changed several times during this job. Fixing wall depth and duct routes early avoids rework.
- **Security and automation.** Roll shutters, security gates and a blister-pack room with its own power were added during construction. Put them in the first scope.
- **Roof penetrations.** On a new building under a roof warranty, plan for the original roofer to make every penetration.
- **Fail-safe controls.** Agree what happens on a fault, such as shutdown and a strobe, before the controls are designed.

Our [NAPRA cleanroom requirements](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/napra-cleanroom-requirements/) guide lists the numbers these rooms are built to, and our [hazardous drug compounding room](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) page covers this type of room in more detail.

## FAQ

### Is the Coastal Care suite set up for hazardous drugs?
Yes. The clean room is held negative to its anteroom, with low-level exhaust, which is the set-up NAPRA describes for hazardous sterile compounding.

### How many air changes does the clean room get?
30 air changes an hour, through HEPA laminar-flow diffusers.

### What happens if the air system fails?
The system shuts down and a red strobe flashes outside the anteroom.

### Did you design the pharmacy?
No. An interior designer drew the plans, and we were construction manager. We also offer [compounding pharmacy design build](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/compounding-pharmacy-design-build/) under one contract.
