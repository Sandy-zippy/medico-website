# Compounding lab design: non-sterile rooms and full labs

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

A compounding lab is the set of rooms where a pharmacy prepares medicines on site. It can be a single non-sterile room built to NAPRA Level A, B or C, or a full facility that combines non-sterile rooms with sterile clean rooms, gowning and storage. We design and build both, for private compounding pharmacies and central compounding facilities in BC and Alberta.

## What are NAPRA Level A, B and C rooms?

**Three levels of non-sterile compounding space: a designated area (A), a separate ventilated room (B), and a separate negative-pressure room with containment (C).**

NAPRA's 2018 non-sterile model standard, clarified in January 2022, sets the levels by how complex and how hazardous the preparations are ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Nonsterile-Preparations-EN-March-2018-CLAR-Jan-2022.pdf)).

| Level | What it covers | What the room needs |
|---|---|---|
| A | Simple and moderate compounds, as defined in USP chapter 795 | A separate space designated for compounding, with a hot and cold sink in or close to the area (s. 5.4.1) |
| B | Complex compounds, and small or occasional quantities that need ventilation, such as hormones | A separate, well-ventilated room; may need a ventilated containment device (s. 8.2) |
| C | NIOSH Group 1 hazardous drugs, WHMIS health hazards, large quantities of Group 2 or 3 ingredients | A physically separated, dedicated room, negative to surrounding rooms, externally vented through HEPA, with a C-PEC and an eyewash; room and C-PEC certified every 6 months (ss. 8.3, 9.1, 9.2, 9.6) |

Saskatchewan's regulator adds a Level C minimum of 12 ACPH and describes a Level B room as fully enclosed with sealed doors and windows ([SCPP chart](https://saskpharm.ca/document/4820/CompEX_RefChart-Physical_Reqts.pdf)). The NAPRA 2018 text itself does not state an ACPH figure for the room.

## Does BC require the NAPRA non-sterile standard?

**Not in bylaw yet. CPBC's PODSA bylaw s. 23.7 adopts only the two sterile standards, so BC has no non-sterile facility deadline today.**

The College's compounding page and bylaws cover sterile compounding only ([CPBC bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf); [CPBC compounding](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1562)). That does not make a hazardous non-sterile room optional in practice. WorkSafeBC requires a ventilated enclosure for mixing and preparing specific hazardous drugs, and an exposure control plan, for every employer ([WorkSafeBC](https://worksafebc.com/about-us/news-events/announcements/2023/November/new-hazardous-drug-requirements-come-into-effect-december-1)).

In Alberta, the college publishes its own non-sterile compounding FAQ for pharmacies, and owners there should design to it ([ACP non-sterile FAQ](https://abpharmacy.ca/wp-content/uploads/Compounding_Non-sterileFAQ.pdf)).

## What is changing with the new NAPRA documents?

**NAPRA's Board has accepted new combined non-sterile and sterile model documents, but they are not published or in effect. Each regulator sets its own timeline.**

NAPRA consulted on the new documents from 13 January to 15 March 2026 and reported in November 2026 that its Board had accepted them, adding that publication "does not mean that they are in effect" ([NAPRA update](https://www.napra.ca/update-on-napras-model-documents-for-pharmacy-regulatory-authority-use-on-non-sterile-and-sterile-compounding-standards/)). The consultation draft sets Level C negative pressure at "at least -2.5 Pa relative to adjacent areas" (s. 3.4.1) and hazardous drug storage at at least 12 ACPH (s. 4.4.5) ([NAPRA draft](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CONSULTATION-DOCUMENT-Model-Documents-for-PRA-Use-Compounding-Standards-Jan26.pdf)).

Pharmacy groups warned the drafts could mean "infrastructure modifications" ([Neighbourhood Pharmacies](https://neighbourhoodpharmacies.ca/proposed-compounding-standards-could-risk-patient-access)). If you are building a Level B or C room now, we suggest sizing the exhaust route, the room pressure controls and the electrical capacity so the room can be tightened later without opening walls. The final values will be whatever your regulator adopts.

## What does a Level C hazardous non-sterile room involve?

**Sealed walls and doors, its own air supply and HEPA exhaust to the outside, negative pressure, a containment hood, an eyewash and low return grilles.**

At [Humble Compounding](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/humble-compounding/) in Langley, a combined pharmacy and clinic, the non-sterile hazardous compounding room is held at negative pressure, with a HEPA flow hood and an eyewash. The design called for 12 air changes an hour, with its own fan coil, a HEPA ceiling supply and an exhaust fan pushing air through a HEPA filter to a new louvre. That one small room drove most of the mechanical scope for the whole pharmacy. It is a non-sterile room, not a clean room.

| Element | Why it matters on site |
|---|---|
| Exhaust route | Needs a wall or roof path and landlord approval early |
| Dedicated air | Keeps the pressure stable when the rest of the suite's HVAC cycles |
| Low grilles | Returns and exhaust near the floor, away from the work zone |
| Eyewash | Required at Level C; needs water, a drain and tempered supply where specified |
| Sealed envelope | Doors, penetrations and ceiling sealed so the room can hold negative pressure |

## How do non-sterile rooms fit into a full compounding lab?

**As separate zones on the same air and exhaust plan, usually linked to the sterile rooms by pass-throughs, with hazardous and non-hazardous work kept apart.**

The [Coastal Care Compounding Lab](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/coastal-care-compounding-lab/) in Surrey, in progress, shows the larger version. In a light-industrial unit of about 3,388 sq ft, a factory-built modular pod holds a gowning room, non-hazardous and hazardous sterile anterooms and clean rooms, and non-sterile and hazardous non-sterile rooms, with interlocked doors and a pass-through. As general contractor for everything around the pod, we handle the structure for the mezzanine air handler, slab trenching, the electrical service, exhaust and fresh air, sprinklers, washrooms and the staff room.

We have also priced a larger compounding centre expansion of about 6,000 sq ft and more, with sterile hazardous and non-hazardous rooms, for another design team. Labs at that scale behave like small industrial projects: service upgrades, structure for air handlers and roof equipment matter as much as the clean rooms.

## FAQ

### Do hormone compounders need a separate room?
Under NAPRA, small or occasional quantities that need ventilation, such as hormones, fall under Level B: a separate, well-ventilated room that may need a ventilated containment device.

### Does a Level C room need external ventilation?
Yes. NAPRA requires Level C rooms to be externally vented through HEPA and held negative relative to surrounding rooms.

### Does a Level C room need an eyewash?
Yes. NAPRA says a Level C room must contain an eyewash station.

### How often is a Level C room certified?
NAPRA requires the room and its C-PEC to be certified every 6 months.

### When will BC adopt the new NAPRA non-sterile rules?
No date has been set. NAPRA's Board accepted the documents in 2026, but each provincial regulator decides when, and whether, they take effect.

### Can a compounding lab go in a light-industrial unit?
Yes. Our Coastal Care Compounding Lab is in one. Check early that the structure, slab, electrical service and roof can carry the air handling and exhaust the rooms need.
