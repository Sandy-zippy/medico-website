# Modular vs built-in pharmacy clean rooms

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

A modular pharmacy clean room is a factory-built panel system assembled inside your space. A built-in clean room is framed, boarded and finished on site. Modular usually costs more up front and installs faster; built-in gives more freedom of layout. Either way, the air handling, exhaust, structure and services around the room are built on site, and that is where much of the cost sits. We build both.

## What is the difference between modular and built-in clean rooms?

**Modular rooms arrive as engineered wall, ceiling and door panels from a supplier. Built-in rooms are framed on site with studs, board and cleanable finishes.**

A built-in (stick-built) room uses steel studs, gypsum or FRP-faced walls, a sealed ceiling, coved flooring and site-installed doors. A modular room uses prefinished panels, often with integrated doors, windows, pass-throughs and a ceiling grid designed for HEPA units. NAPRA sets performance, not a construction method: both have to hold the pressure cascade, air changes and ISO class, and both are certified the same way ([NAPRA hazardous sterile standard](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf)).

Modular rooms are a real option in Canadian hospitals, not only in private labs. A VCH tender for the qathet General Hospital IV room, listed at $2.9M and later cancelled, named a modular supplier ([qathet listing](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/5793237-vch-qathet-general-hospital-powell-river-pharmacy-iv-room-upgrades&find_loc=BC-V8A-5R3)). Sunnybrook in Ontario listed a "Modular Pharmacy Cleanroom Project" at $300,000 ([Sunnybrook listing](https://projects.constructconnect.com/details/7289186-sunnybrook-modular-pharmacy-cleanroom-project&find_loc=-M4N-3M5)).

## How do cost and schedule compare?

**Published US figures put modular at about $50 to $150 per sq ft more than built-in, but 25 to 40 percent faster to build.**

| Factor | Modular | Built-in | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | About US$50 to $150/sf more | Lower | Terrapin (US, 2026) |
| Build speed | 25 to 40 percent faster | Slower, more wet trades | Terrapin (US, 2026) |
| Overrun likelihood | 13 percent | 60 percent (drywall stick-built) | Restore Health (US consultant) |
| Depreciation period | 7 to 15 years | 30 to 39 years | Restore Health (US) |
| Maintenance | Higher-grade materials, higher upfront cost | Repairs can mean compounding stops | ProPharma (vendor) |
| Layout freedom | Set by the panel system | Fits any room shape | Our view |

These are US sources and include vendor and consultant views. Use them as direction, not as a Canadian price ([Terrapin](https://terrapincg.com/news/cleanroom-construction-cost-per-square-foot-2026); [Restore Health](https://restorehealthconsulting.squarespace.com/news/ryf8li6beqc0pbmtiozl1o6raw659z); [ProPharma](https://www.propharmacleanrooms.com/news/modular-systems-vs-stick-built)).

Terrapin also puts HVAC and filtration at 35 to 55 percent of a cleanroom budget. That share does not go away with a modular room. Our [pharmacy clean room cost](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/pharmacy-clean-room-cost/) guide sets these figures beside Canadian tender values.

## What does the general contractor still build around a modular pod?

**Most of the building work: structure for the air handler, slab work, electrical service, exhaust and fresh air, sprinklers and washrooms.**

The [Coastal Care Compounding Lab](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/coastal-care-compounding-lab/) in Surrey, in progress, is our working example. A factory-built pod with a gowning room, non-hazardous and hazardous anterooms and clean rooms, non-sterile rooms, interlocked doors and a pass-through sits inside a light-industrial unit of about 3,388 sq ft. As general contractor for everything around it, we are building:

- structure to carry the mezzanine air handler
- slab trenching and re-pouring for drains
- the electrical service upgrade
- exhaust and fresh air to the outside
- sprinkler drops into the pod ceiling
- washrooms and the staff room

The pod supplier's scope on that job excluded items such as permits, final certification, HEPA certification, roof and wall penetrations, exhaust and fresh-air ducts, and hoods. The supplier's drawings also left it to the client to confirm the slab could carry the pod and met a flatness tolerance. None of that is unusual. It means an owner should compare the full installed cost, pod plus the work around it, and not the pod quote alone.

## When does modular make sense?

**When speed matters, when the room may move or grow, when a live area must avoid wet trades, or when layouts are standard.**

In a hospital, modular panels cut wet trades and dust inside an infection control zone and can shorten the shutdown window, which helps when the pharmacy has to keep compounding during work. Built-in makes more sense when the space is irregular, when the clean room is small and simple, or when the owner wants to match finishes across a larger renovation. In a live hospital, either method still runs under CSA Z317.13 infection control during construction ([Interior Health toolkit](https://www.interiorhealth.ca/sites/default/files/PDFS/infection-prevention-control-construction-projects.pdf)).

| Situation | Usually leans |
|---|---|
| Lease with a short fit-out window | Modular |
| Hospital suite next to a working IV room | Modular, or built-in with strict phasing |
| Irregular room shape or low ceiling | Built-in |
| Small single clean room in a larger renovation | Built-in |
| Owner expects to expand or relocate | Modular |

## What should you check before choosing?

**Ceiling height and slab, the route for exhaust and fresh air, electrical capacity, where the air handler goes, and exactly what the supplier excludes.**

We check five things on a walk-through:

1. **Structure and slab.** Can the floor carry the pod and the air handler, and is it flat enough? Does a mezzanine or roof need reinforcing?
2. **Exhaust and fresh air.** Is there a practical route out, and will the landlord or strata approve it?
3. **Electrical.** Is the service big enough for the air handler, HEPA units and controls?
4. **Scope gaps.** Which items are in the supplier's price and which fall to the contractor?
5. **Certification.** Who books the certifier, and when? See [pharmacy cleanroom certification](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/).

Room areas can also drop slightly between a concept plan and the final pod drawings as panel thicknesses are fixed, so confirm working sizes before ordering equipment.

## FAQ

### Is a modular clean room accepted by the College of Pharmacists?
NAPRA and CPBC set performance requirements, not a construction method. A modular room has to meet the same ISO class, pressure and air change targets and pass the same certification.

### Is modular cheaper?
Usually not up front. US sources put modular at about US$50 to $150 per sq ft more than built-in, but faster to build.

### Does a modular clean room need its own HVAC?
Yes. The pod still needs an air handler, HEPA supply, exhaust and fresh air, and the building has to carry and feed that equipment.

### Can a modular pod go in a light-industrial unit?
Yes. Our Coastal Care Compounding Lab is a modular pod in a light-industrial unit, with structure, services and exhaust built around it.

### Who certifies a modular clean room?
An independent certifier, the same as a built-in room. The C-PEC is certified every 6 months under NAPRA, and the room is tested at each facility certification.
