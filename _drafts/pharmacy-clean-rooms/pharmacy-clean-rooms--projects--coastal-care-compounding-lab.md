# Coastal Care compounding lab, Surrey (in progress)

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

The Coastal Care compounding lab is a dedicated compounding facility of about 3,388 sq ft in a light-industrial unit in Surrey, now in progress. A factory-built modular pod holds its sterile and non-sterile rooms, hazardous and non-hazardous. As general contractor, we build everything around the pod: structure, slab, power, exhaust, fresh air, sprinklers, washrooms and the staff room.

## What is the Coastal Care compounding lab?

**A purpose-built compounding facility, separate from the retail pharmacy, with sterile clean rooms and non-sterile rooms in one modular pod inside a light-industrial unit.**

Coastal Care already runs the [sterile suite we built in 2020](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/projects/coastal-care-sterile-suite/) at its pharmacy. The unit, a former office fit-out, has high ceilings, a loading door and a mezzanine, so the pod sits on the main floor with mechanical equipment above.

## What rooms are in the clean room pod?

**A gowning room leads to two sides: a non-hazardous anteroom and clean room, and a hazardous anteroom and clean room. Two non-sterile rooms sit beyond.**

| Room | Purpose | Air |
|---|---|---|
| Gowning room | Shared entry and garbing | Positive |
| Non-hazardous anteroom | Transition to the non-hazardous clean room | ISO 7, positive |
| Non-hazardous clean room | Non-hazardous sterile preparations | ISO 7, positive |
| Hazardous anteroom | Transition to the hazardous clean room | ISO 7, negative |
| Hazardous clean room | Hazardous sterile preparations | ISO 7, negative |
| Non-sterile room | Non-hazardous non-sterile compounding | Positive |
| Hazardous non-sterile room | Hazardous non-sterile compounding | Negative |

From the 2025 pod drawings. Values may change before certification.

The pod has seven interlocked single-leaf doors, plus a pass-through to the non-sterile rooms. Separate hazardous and non-hazardous clean rooms follow NAPRA's hazardous sterile standard, which says the two "must have two clean rooms" ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf)).

## What does our scope cover?

**Everything outside the pod that the pod needs to work: structure, slab, power, exhaust and fresh air, sprinklers, roof work, washrooms, staff space and finishes.**

- **Demolition and structure.** The old office fit-out is removed, and the existing mezzanine is reinforced with new steel posts, beams and footings so it can carry the clean room air handler and the water heater.
- **Slab.** Trenching and re-pouring for new drains, and a flat floor for the pod.
- **Electrical.** A service upgrade with a new transformer and panel.
- **Exhaust and fresh air.** Exhaust fans for the hoods and clean rooms, and a fresh-air intake.
- **Roof and sprinklers.** A condenser and rooftop unit on sleepers, and sprinkler drops into the pod ceiling.
- **Support rooms.** An accessible washroom, a staff washroom and a staff room with a kitchen on the mezzanine, and an emergency drench hose and eyewash.
- **Finishes and millwork** outside the pod.

## How does a pod fit inside an existing shell?

**The shell comes first: a flat slab, structure for equipment, roof openings, power and exhaust routes. The pod goes in last, onto a finished floor.**

A modular pod brings its own walls, ceiling, doors and air handler, but not the building around it. The supplier's notes leave the slab to the buyer: confirm it "has sufficient strength to resist the load of the cleanroom," with floor variations under 3/8 in per 10 ft.

The supplier's scope also excluded permits, final certification, roof and wall penetrations, HEPA certification, refrigerant piping, exhaust and fresh-air ducts, and hoods. On this job, the items outside the pod package turned into the largest changes. When comparing modular quotes, price the full installed cost: pod plus building work.

Our site schedule runs mezzanine work, rooftop equipment, flooring, final clean and paint, then the pod install.

## What are we learning so far?

**Price the pod supplier's exclusions up front, expect room areas to shrink slightly as panels are fixed, and allow for landlord review.**

- **Exclusions become changes.** List what the pod package leaves out and price it before contract.
- **Areas shrink.** Room areas dropped by about 2 to 3 percent between the concept and the final pod drawings as panel thicknesses were fixed.
- **Strata review.** In a business park, a strata review sat between the permit and tender issues.

## What happens next?

**Commissioning of the air system, then independent certification of the rooms and C-PECs, then the College paperwork before the lab is used.**

In BC, the College of Pharmacists asks for inspection evidence "no later than 14 days after the completion date" of a layout change ([CPBC](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758)). See our [pharmacy cleanroom certification](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/) page.

## FAQ

### Is the Coastal Care compounding lab finished?
Not yet. It is in progress, and we will update this case study at completion.

### Who supplied the clean room pod?
A specialist modular clean room manufacturer supplied the pod under its own contract. We are general contractor for the building work around it.

### Who will certify the rooms?
An independent certifier tests the rooms and C-PECs after we commission the air system.

### Does the lab do sterile and non-sterile compounding?
Yes. It has hazardous and non-hazardous sterile clean rooms and two non-sterile rooms, one for hazardous work.
