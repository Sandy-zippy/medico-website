# Humble Compounding, Langley: a hazardous non-sterile compounding room

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

At Humble Compounding in Langley, we built a sealed, negative-pressure room for non-sterile hazardous compounding inside a combined pharmacy and clinic of about 1,600 sq ft. It was built in 2026, and our role was construction, working from an interior designer's drawings. The room has its own fan coil and heat pump, HEPA filtered supply, HEPA filtered exhaust to outdoors, a flow hood with HEPA filters and a deluge eyewash. It is a non-sterile compounding room, not a clean room.

## What did Humble Compounding need?

**A compounding pharmacy and four-room clinic in one suite, with a sealed room where staff can safely compound hazardous non-sterile preparations.**

Humble is a pharmacist-led pharmacy that makes customized medications, alongside a clinic with four patient rooms. The owners took two combined ground-floor units in a two-storey sprinklered commercial building in Murrayville. The main technical job was one small room: a contained space for hazardous non-sterile work. The full fit-out is covered in our [Humble Compounding Pharmacy case study](/portfolio-2/humble-compounding-pharmacy-langley-bc/).

## What is in the hazardous compounding room?

**A sealed room about 10 by 8.5 ft, held slightly negative to the pharmacy, with its own air system, HEPA filtration and low grilles.**

| Feature | Design |
|---|---|
| Size | About 10 ft 4 in by 8 ft 6 in (about 85 sq ft) |
| Pressure | -0.01 in. water column (about -2.5 Pa) to the pharmacy |
| Air changes | 12 an hour |
| Heating and cooling | Dedicated one-ton fan coil with its own heat pump |
| Supply | HEPA fan filter unit in the ceiling |
| Exhaust | Fan on a variable speed drive, through a HEPA filter, to a new outdoor louvre |
| Grilles | Return and exhaust grilles low, about 12 in above the floor |
| Containment | Flow hood with double HEPA filters |
| Safety and cleaning | Deluge eyewash with its own drain, dishwasher, hand sink |

From the mechanical and electrical drawings, 2026. Sizes are scaled and approximate.

Negative pressure means air leaks into the room, not out, so powders and vapours stay contained. Low grilles pull air down and away from the breathing zone.

## How does the room compare with NAPRA Level C?

**It includes the main features NAPRA lists for Level C: a separate room, negative pressure, HEPA external venting, containment and an eyewash.**

NAPRA's 2018 non-sterile standard describes a Level C room as "physically separated," under negative pressure "relative to surrounding rooms," vented externally through HEPA, with a C-PEC, and says it "must contain an eyewash station" ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Nonsterile-Preparations-EN-March-2018-CLAR-Jan-2022.pdf)). BC has not adopted that standard in bylaw ([PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)), but WorkSafeBC requires "a ventilated enclosure" for mixing and preparing specific hazardous drugs ([WorkSafeBC](https://worksafebc.com/about-us/news-events/announcements/2023/November/new-hazardous-drug-requirements-come-into-effect-december-1)). NAPRA's 2026 draft also gives Level C rooms at least -2.5 Pa ([NAPRA draft](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CONSULTATION-DOCUMENT-Model-Documents-for-PRA-Use-Compounding-Standards-Jan26.pdf)), close to this room's design. The pharmacy's compounding work and the College decide what is required.

## How does the rest of the suite work?

**Separate storefront doors for pharmacy and clinic, self-closing gates between public and work areas, wet rooms at the rear, and reused base-building systems.**

The pharmacy entrance has an automatic opener. Gates separate the public from the dispensary and from the clinic. The compounding room, staff room and washrooms sit at the rear near the existing drain stacks, which keeps plumbing runs short. The two existing fan coils and the water heater were reused, and the electrical panel was relocated rather than replaced. Lighting runs on two zones so the pharmacy and clinic can keep their own hours.

## What would we tell another pharmacist planning a compounding room?

**Plan the compounding room first, because it sets the mechanical budget. Find the exhaust route, then design the rest of the suite around it.**

- **Size it early.** A room of about 85 to 90 sq ft with its own air system is a mechanical project in its own right.
- **Find the exhaust route.** Here, exhaust runs through a HEPA filter to a new louvre. Confirm the landlord allows it before you sign a lease.
- **Leave time for permits.** In the Township of Langley, sprinkler changes need their own permit, and inspectors expect an engineer's seismic sign-off for ceiling and mechanical work.
- **Think ahead.** With new NAPRA standards coming, a room built with negative pressure and external venting now is easier to bring up to a future standard.

## FAQ

### Is the Humble compounding room a clean room?
No. It is a non-sterile hazardous compounding room. It is not ISO classified and is not used for sterile compounding.

### What pressure is the room held at?
About -2.5 Pa (0.01 in. water column) relative to the pharmacy.

### Where does the exhaust go?
Through a HEPA filter to a new outdoor louvre, driven by a dedicated exhaust fan.

### Can a hazardous compounding room fit in a small pharmacy?
Yes. This one is about 85 sq ft in a suite of about 1,600 sq ft that also holds a clinic.
