# Pharmacy cleanroom certification, commissioning and handover

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

Pharmacy cleanroom certification is the independent testing that proves a new or rebuilt clean room and its hoods meet NAPRA: air changes, pressures, HEPA filter integrity and particle counts, with hoods certified to CETA guides. Commissioning comes first and proves the HVAC, controls and alarms work as designed. We plan both from the start of design, book the certifier early, and hand over a pack your pharmacy team and the college can use.

## What is the difference between commissioning and certification?

**Commissioning proves the building systems work as designed. Certification is an independent test proving the rooms and hoods meet the standard before compounding starts.**

| | Commissioning | Certification |
|---|---|---|
| Who does it | Builder, mechanical and controls trades, balancing agency, engineer | Independent certifier |
| What it covers | Air balancing, air handlers, exhaust fans, building controls, pressure monitors, alarms, door interlocks | Air changes, room pressures, HEPA leak tests, particle counts, hood certification |
| When | After the systems are installed and running | After commissioning, before the room is used, then every 6 months |
| Result | Balancing report, controls checkout, alarm test records | Certification reports for rooms and hoods |

A typical hospital clean room mechanical scope includes dedicated air handlers, controls upgrades and "system balancing and commissioning", as on one Ontario hospital project ([Kelson, SHN](https://acceptance.kelson.on.ca/projects/project/5227)).

## What does NAPRA require to be tested?

**Hoods certified every 6 months to CETA guides, HEPA filters tested at each certification, particle counts every 6 months, and yearly pressure instrument calibration.**

| Item | Requirement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hoods (C-PECs) | Certified "every 6 months", to CETA "CAG-003, CAG-005 and CAG-002-2006" | NAPRA hazardous sterile, s. 5.3.3.1 |
| Hazardous drug hoods | Class II or III biological safety cabinet or CACI, running "24 hours a day" | NAPRA hazardous sterile, s. 5.3.3.1 |
| HEPA filters | Tested at each facility certification | NAPRA hazardous sterile |
| Particle counts | At least every 6 months | NAPRA hazardous sterile |
| Pressure instruments | Calibrated "at least once a year" | NAPRA hazardous sterile, s. 5.3.2.9 |
| Pressure monitoring | "measured continuously, and an alarm system must be in place" | CPBC Sterile Compounding Review |
| Air and surface sampling | "at least every 6 months" | CPBC Sterile Compounding Review |
| Room temperature | "less than or equal to 20°C" | CPBC Sterile Compounding Review |

Sources: [NAPRA hazardous sterile model standards](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf); [CPBC 5287](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf).

For non-sterile Level C rooms, the NAPRA non-sterile standard requires the room and its hood to be certified every 6 months ([NAPRA non-sterile, s. 9.6](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Nonsterile-Preparations-EN-March-2018-CLAR-Jan-2022.pdf)).

## Why do clean rooms fail certification, and how do we prevent it?

**Leaky envelopes, unbalanced air, late exhaust route changes and untested alarms. Most failures are set up in design and rough-in, not on test day.**

What we do on site to get a first-time pass:
- **Seal the envelope.** Gasketed or caulked ceiling tiles and lights, sealed penetrations and tight doors hold pressure. A modular supplier's own drawings for a lab we are building now call for sealed supply ductwork and low-leakage dampers at every HEPA filter.
- **Fix the exhaust route early.** On our 2020 sterile suite, the clean room exhaust was drawn only from grilles 6 in above the floor, and the walls were deepened to hold the ducts. The exhaust route changed several times during the job. Size walls for low-level exhaust in the first drawing issue.
- **Test the failure mode.** On that suite, if any part of the system fails it shuts down and a red strobe flashes outside the anteroom. We test every alarm and door interlock before the certifier arrives.
- **Hold temperature.** The 20°C limit depends on cooling capacity with every hood and fridge running. Check it in commissioning, not after opening.
- **Read the supplier's exclusions.** On our modular pod project, the room supplier's scope excludes permits, final certification and HEPA certification. Those items fall to the owner or to whoever the contract names, so the certifier is engaged by the owner or as the contract sets out. We make sure each one is assigned and priced before work starts.

## How long does certification take, and when should it be booked?

**The site visit is short, often hours per room, but plan weeks for sampling results, retests and college evidence. Book the certifier during design.**

One certifier's article gives about 1.5 to 2 hours per biological safety cabinet, and says a large suite with several ISO 5 devices "can take well beyond three hours" ([TRC Healthcare](https://trchealthcare.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/How-Long-Should-Cleanroom-Cert-Really-Take_July-2025.pdf)). A US builder recommends allowing 3 to 4 weeks for certification in the schedule and bringing the certifier in early ([JP Cullen](https://jpcullen.com/?p=2689)).

| Step | Timing in the schedule |
|---|---|
| Certifier reviews the design | During design, before tender |
| Commissioning and air balancing | After systems are running, before certification |
| Certification visit | After commissioning; hours per room |
| Sampling results and any retests | Days to weeks after the visit |
| CPBC evidence (BC) | Within 14 days of completion ([CPBC](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/1758)) |

In BC, NSF lists accredited biosafety cabinet field certifiers by province ([NSF listings](https://info.nsf.org/Certified/biosafety-certifier/listings.asp)). For timing across the whole project, see [how long a clean room takes](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/guides/cleanroom-project-timeline/).

## What should the handover pack include?

**Certification reports, balancing and controls records, calibration certificates, alarm test results, as-built drawings for the college diagram, and maintenance information for every system.**

| Document | Why you need it |
|---|---|
| Room and hood certification reports | Proof of compliance for the college and your own records |
| Air balancing report | Baseline air changes and flows for future troubleshooting |
| Controls checkout and alarm tests | Shows pressure alarms and interlocks work |
| Pressure monitor calibration certificates | NAPRA requires yearly calibration; this starts the cycle |
| As-built drawings | The scaled diagram for the college and future renovations |
| Filter and equipment schedules | So your maintenance team orders the right HEPA and prefilters |
| Maintenance log, started | The CPBC hospital checklist asks for a log with hood maintenance and certification ([CPBC](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/7_Forms/7-3_Pharmacy/9120-HOS_PY_Preopening_Inspection_Report_Photo_Checklist.pdf)) |

The pharmacy's environmental monitoring plan and its baseline sampling belong to the pharmacy, but we time construction cleaning and the certifier's visit so the baseline starts on a clean room.

## FAQ

### How often do pharmacy clean rooms need recertification?
NAPRA requires hood certification and particle counts at least every 6 months, and CPBC checks air and surface sampling at the same interval.

### Who certifies pharmacy clean rooms?
An independent certifier. NAPRA cites CETA guides for hood certification. Pick a certifier with pharmacy compounding experience.

### Does the builder hire the certifier?
Either can. Owners often hire the certifier directly; we schedule and coordinate the visit either way.

### What happens if a room fails?
The certifier reports the failed tests, we fix the cause, and the room is retested before use.

### Do non-sterile rooms need certification?
NAPRA Level C rooms and their hoods need certification every 6 months under the non-sterile standard.
