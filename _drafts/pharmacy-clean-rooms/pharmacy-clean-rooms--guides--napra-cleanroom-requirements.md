# NAPRA cleanroom requirements: what the rooms must have

*By Dal Riarh, President and CEO, The Medico Group. Last updated 27 November 2026.*

NAPRA's model standards for sterile compounding set the room numbers for a pharmacy clean room in Canada: ISO Class 7 air, at least 30 air changes an hour for hazardous suites, set pressure differences, and exhaust to outdoors for hazardous work. Provincial colleges make them binding. In BC, the College of Pharmacists' bylaws require both sterile standards, and its inspectors check the built rooms against them.

## Which NAPRA standards apply to a pharmacy clean room?

**Two sterile model standards apply: one for non-hazardous and one for hazardous preparations. Provincial colleges adopt them, and BC made both mandatory in 2022.**

NAPRA, the national association of pharmacy regulators, writes model standards. Each provincial college decides whether and when to adopt them. In BC, section 23.7 of the PODSA bylaws requires pharmacy owners and managers to comply with NAPRA's non-hazardous and hazardous sterile standards "as amended from time to time" ([PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). The College's sterile compounding standards came into effect on July 4, 2022 ([CPBC](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/2694)), after a one-time extension of the facility deadline ([CPBC](https://www.bcpharmacists.org/node/2450)).

In Alberta, the Alberta College of Pharmacy treats the NAPRA model standards as "the minimum requirements" for sterile compounding ([ACP](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/does-your-pharmacy-provide-sterile-compounding/)) and requires approval for pharmacy renovations ([ACP](https://abpharmacy.ca/news/pharmacy-renovations-require-approval-acp/)). Alberta Health Services' facility design requirements also list sterile compounding pharmacies as restricted areas ([AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)). Confirm the adopted version with your college before design starts.

## What does a hazardous sterile compounding suite need?

**An ISO 7 anteroom and ISO 7 clean room, a negative clean room inside a positive anteroom, 30 air changes an hour, and outdoor exhaust.**

These are the figures a mechanical engineer designs to and a certifier tests against.

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Anteroom air | ISO Class 7 |
| Anteroom pressure | At least +5.0 Pa to the pharmacy next to it (CPBC: ideally 5.0 to 12.5 Pa) |
| Clean room air | ISO Class 7, with the C-PEC providing ISO 5 |
| Clean room pressure | -2.5 Pa to surrounding areas |
| Air changes | At least 30 ACPH in the clean room and the anteroom |
| Exhaust | Clean room return air exhausted to the exterior, taken low on the walls |
| C-PEC | Class II or III biological safety cabinet, or a CACI, running 24 hours a day |
| HD storage room | -2.5 Pa, at least 12 ACPH, all air exhausted outdoors (or HD stock kept in the clean room) |
| Water | No sinks, water sources or drains in the clean room; allowed in the anteroom |
| Temperature | Controlled rooms at or below 20°C |
| Monitoring | Continuous pressure measurement with an alarm; instruments calibrated at least yearly |
| Certification | C-PECs every 6 months to CETA CAG-003, CAG-005 and CAG-002; air and surface sampling at least every 6 months |

Sources: [NAPRA hazardous sterile standard](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Hazardous-Sterile-Preparations-Sep-2016-Revised-b.pdf) and the [CPBC Sterile Compounding Review form](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf), as of November 2026.

NAPRA allows a shared anteroom for hazardous and non-hazardous work, but says "This layout is not recommended," and all air in a shared anteroom must be exhausted outdoors. Hazardous and non-hazardous compounding still need two separate clean rooms. Our [hazardous drug compounding room](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hazardous-compounding/) page covers the build details.

## How is a non-hazardous sterile room different?

**The cascade runs the other way. The clean room sits positive to its anteroom and the anteroom positive to the pharmacy, so air flows outward.**

For non-hazardous sterile preparations, the goal is to keep particles out, so air flows from the clean room outward. The CPBC review form lists an ISO 8 non-hazardous anteroom with at least 20 air changes an hour and at least +5.0 Pa to the pharmacy ([CPBC review form](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/5_Programs/5-2_PRP/5287-PRP_Sterile_Compounding_Review.pdf)). The clean room stays at ISO 7. See our [sterile compounding room](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/sterile-compounding/) page for layouts.

## What do inspectors check after construction?

**The College checks pressures, alarms, temperature, air changes and sampling records, plus photo evidence of the anteroom line, sink, eyewash, pass-through and C-PEC.**

The CPBC Sterile Compounding Review form says "Pressure must be measured continuously, and an alarm system must be in place." It also checks the 20°C limit and the sampling cycle. For hospital pharmacies, the College's pre-opening photo checklist asks for the anteroom demarcation line, a hands-free sink and eyewash, the pass-through, the C-PEC type, HD storage, an incubator or an external lab report, and the C-PEC maintenance log ([CPBC photo checklist](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/7_Forms/7-3_Pharmacy/9120-HOS_PY_Preopening_Inspection_Report_Photo_Checklist.pdf)). Our [pharmacy cleanroom certification](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/certification-commissioning/) page explains the handover pack.

## What do NAPRA's rules say about non-sterile compounding labs?

**NAPRA's 2018 non-sterile standard sets Levels A, B and C. BC has not adopted it in bylaw, and new national standards are coming.**

| Level | What it covers | Room requirement |
|---|---|---|
| A | Simple and moderate compounds | Separate space designated for compounding, with a sink nearby |
| B | Complex compounds, small quantities needing ventilation (hormones, allergenic products) | Separate, well-ventilated room, possibly with a ventilated containment device |
| C | NIOSH Group 1 hazardous drugs and similar | Dedicated room, negative pressure, HEPA external venting, C-PEC, eyewash, certified every 6 months |

Source: [NAPRA non-sterile standard, clarified January 2022](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/NAPRA-Mdl-Stnds-Pharmacy-Compounding-Nonsterile-Preparations-EN-March-2018-CLAR-Jan-2022.pdf).

BC's bylaw lists only the two sterile standards ([PODSA bylaws](https://library.bcpharmacists.org/6_Resources/6-1_Provincial_Legislation/5082-PODSA_Bylaws.pdf)). Hazardous drug rules from WorkSafeBC still apply to every BC employer: since December 1, 2023, "A ventilated enclosure must be used for mixing, preparing, and priming specific hazardous drugs" ([WorkSafeBC](https://worksafebc.com/about-us/news-events/announcements/2023/November/new-hazardous-drug-requirements-come-into-effect-december-1)). Our [compounding lab design](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/non-sterile-compounding-lab/) page covers these rooms.

## Are the new NAPRA compounding standards in effect?

**Not yet. NAPRA's Board accepted new combined documents in November 2026, but they are not published, and each college will set its own start date.**

NAPRA consulted on new non-sterile and sterile model documents from January 13 to March 15, 2026. It reports the documents "have now been accepted by the NAPRA Board," and adds that "even once the new NAPRA documents are published, this does not mean that they are in effect" ([NAPRA](https://www.napra.ca/update-on-napras-model-documents-for-pharmacy-regulatory-authority-use-on-non-sterile-and-sterile-compounding-standards/)). The consultation draft gave Level C rooms "negative pressures of at least -2.5 Pa relative to adjacent areas" and external venting ([NAPRA draft](https://www.napra.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CONSULTATION-DOCUMENT-Model-Documents-for-PRA-Use-Compounding-Standards-Jan26.pdf)). If you are building now, ask your designer to leave room for exhaust and pressure control so a later upgrade is smaller.

## Where do CSA Z8000 and Z317.2 fit?

**Health authorities write CSA Z8000 and Z317.2 into their contracts. NAPRA sets the pharmacy numbers. Designers apply both and use whichever is stricter.**

Fraser Health requires facilities "designed compliant with CSA Z8000," and Alberta Health Services points to Z317.2 Table 1 for HVAC by room type ([Fraser Health TDR](https://www.fraserhealth.ca/-/media/Project/FraserHealth/FraserHealth/Technical-Design-Requirements/Combined/Technical_Design_Requirements_Combined_Sections.pdf), [AHS](https://www.albertahealthservices.ca/assets/healthinfo/ipc/hi-ipc-hcf-design.pdf)). Our [CSA Z8000 guide](/csa-z8000-standard/) explains the standard, and our [hospital pharmacy NAPRA upgrade](/pharmacy-clean-rooms/hospital-pharmacy/) page covers health authority work.

## FAQ

### How many air changes does a NAPRA clean room need?
At least 30 air changes an hour in a hazardous clean room and its anteroom. The CPBC review form lists at least 20 for a non-hazardous anteroom, and a hazardous drug storage room needs at least 12.

### What pressure does a hazardous drug clean room need?
-2.5 Pa relative to surrounding areas, inside an anteroom held at least +5.0 Pa to the pharmacy. The College asks for continuous monitoring with an alarm.

### Can the clean room have a sink?
No. NAPRA says water sources, sinks and drains must not be in a clean room. They are allowed in the anteroom.

### Can hazardous and non-hazardous rooms share an anteroom?
NAPRA allows it but does not recommend it. All air in a shared anteroom must be exhausted outdoors, and you still need two clean rooms.

### Does BC require the NAPRA non-sterile standard?
Not in bylaw as of November 2026. BC's bylaws list only the two sterile standards, though WorkSafeBC hazardous drug rules still apply.
